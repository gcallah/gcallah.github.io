# Gerry Scott

![Gerry Scott](/graphics/GerryScott.jpg)

Tonight, let me tell you about Gerry Scott.

Maybe the first time I met him, but more likely the third or fourth, he was
bartending at a place called Moonshine on Columbia Street in Brooklyn. I ordered
a cider from him in a glass with ice.

He cocked his head a little to one side, and asked me, "How many dicks have you
sucked?" (This was his way of telling me that he thought my drink order was a
little effeminate.)

Later that night, or perhaps a visit or two later, someone asked me, "Have you
ever seen Gerry turn on all the beer taps at once by slamming his head on the
bar?"

I told him "no," while thinking this must be some tall tale: I mean, even if
he *could* do that, why *would* he?

But a few nights later, I got to see it for myself: at some point in the
evening, to express whatever frustrations he had at that moment, Gerry slammed
his head down onto the bar, and every beer tap sprang on at once.

Gradually, I discovered that he was the most injured man who had ever lived,
or the very least, the most injured man I have ever known. He
had had leukemia as a teenager, and had been read his last rites several times.
He mentioned that he had been shot three times and stabbed seven. That sounded
unbelievable, until he lifted up his shirt and showed me his scars. (He had been
the manager of a security company, if you're wondering why all the shooting and
stabbing.)

But these were far from his only injuries. Every few weeks, he would report some
new incident. One time, he had been walking down the street when *another*
pedestrian was hit by a car, flung right into Gerry, which knocked him through a
plate glass window. Another time, he had gone to lift up the bar coffee pot, but
someone had burned coffee beneath the pot, which had melded to the glass bottom,
which was left behind as Gerry lifted the bottomless pot and drenched his chest
and legs with piping hot coffee.

And throughout it all, he was of remarkably good cheer, unlike our mutual friend
Santo, who I will tell you about tomorrow night.

PS: Gerry once forgot Stevie Wonder on stage. I will tell you about that another
night.
