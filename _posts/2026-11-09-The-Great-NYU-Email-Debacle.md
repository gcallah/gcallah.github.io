# The Great NYU Email Debacle

There is a mail list at New York University that reaches every member of the
faculty. Access to this is supposed to be very restricted: not even the faculty
can generate an email to this list, at least in theory.

But one day, somehow, a student, just an ordinary undergraduate student,
managed to gain access and send an email to the list. So the email went out to
all 13,000 faculty members at the university. I don't remember exactly what it
said, but it was something like, "I can't make your class on Thursday: can I
take the test at some other time?"

Now, being the clever fellow I am, I recognized that this mail was not meant
for me, that it went out to a whole mail list, and I just deleted it. But
immediately, my mailbox began filling up. I took a look at what was happening:
one member of the faculty after another was responding to the student, saying,
for instance, "I'm not giving a test on Thursday," or "I'm not even teaching
this semester."

And they were all hitting "REPLY ALL"!

So literally hundreds of my fellow faculty members were responding to a
13,000-person email list, telling all of us that this one student was not in
their class, or something to that effect.

Do you think it ended there? No, it did not. The next wave of emails filling my
inbox came from hundreds of other faculty members, who were mailing the entire
list, telling everyone, "Don't hit 'Reply all'!"

After a few hours, things began to settle down. But it was an amazing few
hours.
